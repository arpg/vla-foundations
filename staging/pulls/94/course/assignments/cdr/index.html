CDR: Critical Design Review

Critical Design Review

Author: Carson Kohlbrenner

My project is called Humanoid Proximity Dreaming. The objective is to teach a humanoid robot to predict what its onboard proximity sensors will observe in the future so that it can plan safe motions. I plan to teach this model using a world model architecture that encodes the sensor states into a latent vector, then a dynamics model autoregressively predicts what tokens come next.

The Architectural Delta

Can your partner state, in one sentence, what bottleneck you are addressing and why the existing approach fails? If they can't, your framing needs work.

One sentence: Engineering the representation of data used to train a world model to handle sparse proximity images may increase sample efficiency of learning contact-dependent tasks.

One paragraph: Predicting contact with nearby objects is difficult with model-based methods when the nearby objects are highly dynamic. A proximity sensing world model that can dream future observations will be more robust to the dynamics found in data instead of explicit modeling. By training a world model on sequences of low-dimensional proximity images instead of camera images, a contact avoidant policy can be trained effectively on a smaller distribution of trajectories since proximity is the salient feature for contact avoidance.

The Architecture

The architecture of the world model is encoder -> dynamics model -> decoder. The encoder is a 3D VQ-VAE that encodes the full set of sensor images into a single vector. The dynamics model is a transformer that predicts the next latent token in time. The decoder is a 3D VQ-VAE that decodes the predicted latent token back into a set of sensor images.

You can see that there is a clear trajectory being predicted, and the model hallucinates when the ball is predicted to already have made contact. To handle this, I need to add a contact token in the sequence to indicate when the ball has made contact. This should act sort of like an EOS token in a language model to signal the end of a sequence.

Evaluation

I will evaluate the model's prediction accuracy on a test set of trajectories. Right now, I can only handle when the robot is stationary, but my stretch goal (which is the same as my architecture lab 2 72 hour delta because I found out this is much harder than I anticipated) is to test prediction accuracy when the robot is moving and provide actions into the dynamics model. I will test against frame-by-frame accuracy as well as consistency accuracy (i.e. even if the trajectories deviated, did they accurately model a ball trajectory). The frame by frame accuracy will just be the average L2 distance between the predicted and ground truth sensor images, but I am still figuring out how I could evaluate consistency accuracy.

The Hard Question

Your partner asks the one question they think will come up during the CDR that you are not prepared to answer. Write it down.

From Ari: "How do you know that the model isn't just learning to look at a specific sensor to predict the next frames?"

Response: I did not know how to answer this question when I was asked! After thinking about it, I believe the best way to prevent this sort of learned behavior would be to mask out sensors randomly during training. This is the same strategy used in the JEPA paper and it may be the best way to prevent the model from learning to look at a specific sensor to predict the next frames.
