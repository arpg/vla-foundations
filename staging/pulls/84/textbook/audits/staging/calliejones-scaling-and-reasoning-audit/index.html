Scaling and Reasoning in LLMsDRAFT

Scaling & Reasoning Through Verification and Test-Time Compute

DeepSeekMath (GRPO), DeepSeek-R1, Cosmos-Reason1

By Callie Jones

Problem Domain & Taxonomy

Reasoning in large language models is often evaluated based on the ability to generate structured intermediate steps that lead to correct outputs. Early work emphasized the presence of reasoning traces, but more recent results suggest that structure alone does not guarantee correctness. Instead, reasoning performance depends on how models evaluate, compare, and refine multiple candidate solutions. This reframes reasoning as a process involving search and selection rather than a single forward pass.

The papers examined here explore this shift through different mechanisms. DeepSeekMath focuses on mathematical reasoning tasks where correctness is verifiable and introduces a reinforcement learning objective that compares groups of sampled outputs. DeepSeek-R1 extends this paradigm and demonstrates that reasoning behaviors can emerge when models are optimized to prefer correct outcomes, especially when multiple trajectories are sampled. Cosmos-Reason1 expands reasoning into multimodal domains, where correctness depends on consistency with physical and temporal constraints rather than purely symbolic answers.

What We're Trying To Understand

Do improvements in reasoning come from better generation of reasoning trajectories, or from mechanisms that evaluate and select among multiple candidates?

Summary of Findings

  • Reasoning quality is not determined by a single generated chain, but by comparing multiple candidates
  • Reinforcement learning enables models to prefer correct reasoning trajectories over incorrect ones
  • Test-time compute acts as a scaling mechanism by allowing search over possible solutions
  • Multimodal reasoning introduces new forms of verification based on physical consistency rather than symbolic correctness

Context

Initial work on reasoning in language models relied on prompting strategies such as Chain-of-Thought, where models are encouraged to produce intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer. This approach demonstrated that models contain latent reasoning capabilities that can be elicited through structured prompts. However, these methods do not provide a mechanism for verifying whether the reasoning steps are correct.

As a result, reasoning quality remained dependent on the model’s ability to generate a single correct trajectory. Subsequent work introduced reinforcement learning as a way to directly optimize reasoning quality using reward signals derived from correctness. This shifts reasoning from passive generation to an optimization process that encourages better trajectories over time.

In parallel, test-time compute has emerged as an important factor. Instead of relying on a single output, models can generate multiple candidate solutions and select the most promising one. This effectively treats reasoning as a search problem, where increased sampling and evaluation improve final accuracy.

Taxonomy of Approaches

The reviewed works can be categorized according to how they incorporate verification into reasoning. The first category includes methods such as Chain-of-Thought prompting, where reasoning is generated but not explicitly evaluated. These approaches improve interpretability and sometimes accuracy, but they lack mechanisms to correct errors.

The second category includes training-time verification approaches, such as DeepSeekMath and DeepSeek-R1. These systems use reinforcement learning to optimize reasoning trajectories based on correctness signals. The model learns to prefer reasoning paths that lead to correct answers, effectively internalizing verification.

The third category extends verification into multimodal domains. Cosmos-Reason1 represents this approach by requiring reasoning to align with visual and temporal inputs. Verification is more complex in this setting, since correctness depends on consistency with observed physical behavior rather than discrete answers.

Comparative Architectural Deep-Dive

Objective Functions

DeepSeekMath introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization, a reinforcement learning method designed for reasoning tasks. Instead of estimating absolute value functions, GRPO compares multiple sampled outputs for the same prompt and assigns relative advantages based on their rewards.

For a set of responses with rewards ( r_i ), the mean and standard deviation are computed, and each response is assigned a normalized advantage:

A^i=ri−μrσr\hat{A}_i = \frac{r_i - \mu_r}{\sigma_r}A^i​=σr​ri​−μr​​

This formulation ensures that updates depend on relative performance within the group. Intuitively, outputs that perform better than average are reinforced, while worse-performing outputs are suppressed. The policy is updated using a clipped objective that prevents excessively large updates:

L=E[min⁡(ri(θ)A^i, clip(ri(θ),1−ϵ,1+ϵ)A^i)]L = \mathbb{E} \left[ \min \left( r_i(\theta)\hat{A}_i,\ \text{clip}(r_i(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_i \right) \right]L=E[min(ri​(θ)A^i​, clip(ri​(θ),1−ϵ,1+ϵ)A^i​)]

DeepSeek-R1 applies similar principles but demonstrates that reasoning can emerge even with relatively simple reward signals, such as correctness of the final answer. By repeatedly sampling and reinforcing successful trajectories, the model improves its reasoning over time.

Cosmos-Reason1 differs in that its objective is not purely symbolic. Instead of optimizing for exact answers, it must evaluate whether outputs are consistent with physical and visual inputs, introducing a different form of verification.

Backbone Scaling

The scaling behavior of these systems highlights the importance of factors beyond parameter count. DeepSeekMath shows that curated datasets with verifiable solutions significantly improve reasoning performance, even for smaller models. This indicates that high-quality feedback signals are more important than scale alone.

DeepSeek-R1 demonstrates that increasing the number of sampled reasoning trajectories improves accuracy. By generating multiple candidate solutions and selecting among them, the model effectively increases its chances of producing a correct answer without additional training.

Cosmos-Reason1 introduces multimodal scaling by combining visual and textual inputs. This increases model complexity but enables reasoning about physical processes that cannot be captured through text alone.

Interaction Mechanisms

DeepSeek-R1 relies on transformer-based self-attention to process token sequences, with reasoning behaviors emerging through reinforcement learning. The model generates intermediate reasoning steps that are shaped by reward signals, leading to improved coherence and accuracy.

Cosmos-Reason1 uses early fusion to integrate visual and textual inputs. Visual data is encoded into token representations and processed alongside text, enabling joint reasoning. However, this process introduces challenges related to information compression and alignment across modalities.

The Scaling Frontier & Empirical Trends

Scaling Laws

The results across these papers suggest that reasoning performance scales with the availability of verification signals rather than model size alone.

  • DeepSeekMath demonstrates that access to verifiable data allows smaller models to outperform larger models trained on generic datasets.

  • DeepSeek-R1 highlights the importance of test-time compute. By sampling multiple reasoning trajectories and selecting the best one, the model effectively trades additional computation for improved accuracy.

  • Cosmos-Reason1 shows that scaling multimodal data introduces new challenges, as verification must account for physical consistency rather than symbolic correctness.

Diminishing Returns

Increasing model size alone leads to diminishing improvements in reasoning tasks. Without verification, larger models may generate more fluent but not more accurate reasoning. Reinforcement learning improves performance but depends on reliable reward signals.

In multimodal settings, diminishing returns arise from limitations in representation. Visual information is compressed into tokens, which can result in loss of spatial detail and reduce reasoning accuracy.

Robotic Grounding & Physicality Gap

The Precision Gap

Symbolic reasoning operates at a level of abstraction that does not capture continuous physical dynamics. While models can produce logically consistent explanations, they struggle with precise physical interactions. Cosmos-Reason1 attempts to address this gap by incorporating visual inputs, but discretization introduces limitations.

Benchmark Critique

Most reasoning systems are evaluated on static benchmarks with well-defined answers. While these benchmarks provide clear metrics, they do not reflect real-world complexity. Cosmos-Reason1 introduces more realistic tasks, but these remain limited to offline evaluation.

Engineering Bottlenecks

Key challenges remain in scaling reasoning systems to real-world applications. Information loss during encoding reduces fidelity, particularly for visual inputs. Generating multiple reasoning trajectories increases compute cost. There is also a gap between high-level reasoning and low-level control that limits practical deployment.

Critical Synthesis & The Load Bearing Audit

Load Bearing Assumptions

These approaches assume that reasoning improves when models can evaluate and compare multiple candidate solutions. This depends on the availability of reliable verification signals, which may not exist in all domains.

Foundational vs Marginal Gains

GRPO provides a scalable method for reinforcement learning in reasoning tasks without requiring value function estimation. DeepSeek-R1 demonstrates that reasoning behaviors improve with sampling and selection. Cosmos- Reason1 extends reasoning into multimodal domains, though challenges remain in grounding reasoning in physical reality.

Chain-of-Thought prompting provides useful structure but lacks verification mechanisms, limiting its ability to scale.

Failure Modes

These systems exhibit several failure modes. Models may generate plausible but incorrect reasoning when verification is weak. Multimodal reasoning may fail due to loss of spatial precision. Increased sampling improves accuracy but increases computational cost.

The Next 10,000 GPU-Hour Experiment

A promising direction is to combine reinforcement learning with explicit test-time verification. Models would generate multiple candidate solutions and use external evaluation to select the best outcome. Extending this approach to interactive environments could improve robustness and reliability.
